# Recover a document and check attribution

Use this only when the available text is incomplete, attribution is uncertain or retrieval fails. Check the current tool catalogue; no particular converter or plugin is required.

- Text PDF or office file: try available document conversion, such as MarkItDown. For scanned pages, tables or layout-dependent claims, inspect the relevant pages with available PDF/image tools; use `pdf:pdf` when that procedure is needed.
- Empty JavaScript page: use available browser rendering or `crawl4ai:crawl4ai`. Anti-bot failure: consider `scrapling:scrapling-official`. Read only the procedure actually selected. Make one justified alternative after a failure; do not repeat the same failed request. If that also fails, record the access gap and continue with independent evidence or a qualified conclusion. Revisit only when new evidence indicates a different viable route or the user requests further diagnosis. Troubleshooting the website is not automatically part of researching the question.

Record the exact source URL or non-web identifier/location, tool, failure or partial result, and whether continuous relevant text was recovered. Metadata, a wrapper or contents page is not the document body.

For a load-bearing citation, keep a compact evidence receipt in the working context or existing evidence record: claim; exact source URL or non-web identifier/location; URL origin where applicable (retrieved link, observed navigation/handoff, or manually constructed); tool and check time; relevant recovered passage/data or precise location; retrieval limits and what remains unverified. Link the claim to the source that actually displayed its evidence. A constructed URL is an attempted navigation aid until the relevant result is inspected; its parameters do not prove that the site applied the requested dates, travellers or filters. Do not present it as an observed seller handoff.

Distinguish request completion from evidence recovery. HTTP 200, `isError:false`, a page title, a URL-only heading or an empty extraction supplies no substantive claim support. If rendering recovers the relevant displayed content, review that content instead of treating a failed text extraction as proof of absence. When a citation fails, inspect its original retrieval record before classifying it: an observed source that is inaccessible now, a wrong or unsupported link, and a claim with no recovered support require different corrections. State an unresolved origin as unknown; do not present an unobserved replacement URL as retrieved evidence or invent a past successful read. Diagnose the cause only to the extent supported by observed errors.

Carry those receipts into the bundled [claim/source validator](claim-validation.md). Preserve the actual extracted/rendered text or supported raw MCP result in the study's evidence folder; metadata alone is insufficient. A hash records file consistency and does not authenticate its contents or declared origin. Keep original evidence rather than manufacturing a snapshot to satisfy a check.

Extraction and attribution are separate checks. Before attributing a consequential claim, inspect its passage, section heading and boundaries, nearby byline/date/provenance statement, and printed/PDF page when available. A host or compilation title alone does not authenticate every included work. Keep verified parts of an uncertain source separate from unresolved parts; qualify or omit unsupported attribution.
