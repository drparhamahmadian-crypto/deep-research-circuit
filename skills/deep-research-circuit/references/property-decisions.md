# Property decisions

Use for a property shortlist or purchase decision in any country. Establish the location, intended use, decision stage and constraints. Identify the local ownership, disclosure, planning and tax systems before choosing sources. Early discovery needs proportionate screening; reserve transaction due diligence for serious candidates.

For area selection, compare affordable property types, verified comparable sales, transport/commute, services, physical suitability and recurring ownership costs against the buyer's priorities. Distinguish asking prices, completed sales, area averages and forecasts; aggregate growth does not predict a particular property's return. For affordability or buyer assistance, check current official eligibility, limits and costs. Distinguish loan scenarios from personalised approval, and grants from transaction-tax relief or loan-guarantee schemes. Do not imply eligibility from buyer status alone.

Separate advertisements and price guides from verified sales, ownership/title, contract and current local disclosure records. Match the parcel and boundaries; fences alone do not establish title. Check use restrictions, easements, shared-building or association liabilities, planning/building approvals and relevant hazards, contamination or insurance issues using the responsible local authorities. Connect each issue to the user's intended use and total cost, including applicable taxes, recurring charges and necessary work. A planning map does not establish development permission or physical condition.

State which decisive records, inspections or professional checks remain outstanding and what each would resolve. Do not present a shortlisted property as cleared for purchase. Apply [law-and-policy.md](law-and-policy.md) only to material legal requirements.

Select current primary sources for the actual location: land/title registry, planning and building authority, property regulator, tax authority and relevant hazard records. This is a general research procedure; its local completeness and accuracy must be assessed against those sources.
