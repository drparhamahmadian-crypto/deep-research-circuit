# Other and mixed enquiries

Do not force an unfamiliar subject into an existing specialist workflow. Identify the claims that decide the user's question, the sources competent to establish them, the relevant place/date and plausible contrary evidence. Use a suitable available skill only when its actual method adds a necessary check.

Prefer original records for facts, but use expert interpretation where interpreting those records requires it. Separate official status, empirical performance, personal preference and prediction. Multiple pages derived from one source are not corroboration. For disputed claims, compare the best available evidence on each side rather than counting pages.

For recommendations, preserve the user's constraints, compare credible alternatives and verify changing availability or costs. For mixed questions, attach subject checks only to claims needing them: a product's stock needs shopping verification; a health claim about it may also need scientific appraisal. One primary workflow per assignment does not mean one method must answer every kind of claim.

If evidence is missing, identify the smallest decision-changing enquiry or report the remaining unknown. A tool failure is not evidence about the subject. Do not buy access, invent coverage or indefinitely continue searches that cannot change the answer.
